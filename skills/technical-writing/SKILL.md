# Technical Writing

Write for a specific reader trying to complete a specific task.

1. Identify the reader, prerequisite knowledge, task, and expected outcome.
2. Put the key result or action early; use descriptive headings and a coherent sequence.
3. Prefer concrete verbs, defined terms, short examples, and consistent naming.
4. Separate prerequisites, steps, expected output, troubleshooting, and references.
5. Verify every command, path, API field, version, and claim against current source or a runnable example.
6. Label assumptions and platform differences. Avoid unsupported claims such as "secure", "fast", or "production-ready".
7. Remove repetition, empty adjectives, and instructions that do not change reader action.

Preserve the author's voice where editing existing content. Do not trade accuracy for polish. For reference material, optimize for lookup; for tutorials, optimize for successful completion; for conceptual docs, explain why and where the concept applies.
