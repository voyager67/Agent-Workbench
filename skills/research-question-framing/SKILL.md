# Research Question Framing

Turn curiosity into a question that can be investigated with available evidence and resources.

1. State the phenomenon, target population/system, task, and intended contribution.
2. Separate the research question from a proposed solution or preferred model.
3. Identify what is already known, what is uncertain, and why resolving the uncertainty matters.
4. Define measurable outcomes, baselines, comparison groups, and boundary conditions where applicable.
5. Check feasibility against data access, compute, time, expertise, ethical/legal constraints, and reproducibility.
6. Search for adjacent terminology and prior work before claiming novelty.
7. Propose a small set of falsifiable sub-questions and a stopping criterion.

Deliver a question statement, motivation, scope, hypotheses if justified, evaluation targets, constraints, and open assumptions. Do not claim novelty without a documented literature search. Prefer a smaller question with a credible experiment over a grand claim that cannot be tested.
