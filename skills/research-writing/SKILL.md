# Research Writing

Treat the supplied evidence package as the source of truth.

1. Identify audience, venue/format, contribution, required sections, word/page limits, and disclosure rules.
2. Build an outline that follows the argument: problem, prior work, method, evaluation, results, limitations, and conclusion.
3. Link claims to verified citations, data, code, and figure/table sources as you draft.
4. Preserve exact metric definitions, units, datasets, baselines, and uncertainty. Do not extrapolate beyond evidence.
5. Label measured, simulated, estimated, and illustrative values. If a result is absent, leave a clear placeholder or state that it was not measured.
6. State limitations and threats to validity honestly. Distinguish contribution from implementation details.
7. Verify references, equations, cross-references, figure captions, and venue instructions against current sources.
8. Prepare a claim-to-evidence audit before treating the draft as complete.

Do not fabricate citations, results, authorship, novelty, or peer-review feedback. The human researcher remains responsible for domain correctness, authorship, ethics, and submission decisions.
