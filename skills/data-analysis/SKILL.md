# Data Analysis

1. Clarify the question, unit of analysis, target population, and decision the analysis should inform.
2. Inspect data provenance, schema, types, duplicates, missingness, outliers, and known collection limits.
3. Validate transformations and aggregation logic before interpreting results.
4. Choose descriptive or inferential methods that match the data-generating process and assumptions.
5. Inspect distributions and relevant subgroups; avoid hiding meaningful variation behind averages.
6. Use clear labels, units, sample sizes, and uncertainty where appropriate. Make figures readable and reproducible.
7. Separate correlation from causation and statistical significance from practical importance.
8. Deliver code/notebook steps, key findings, caveats, and the exact data slice analyzed.

Do not modify source data in place. Do not infer sensitive characteristics. Protect private datasets and avoid external upload without permission. Never invent values or present a small exploratory sample as representative without justification.
