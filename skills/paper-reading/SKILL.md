# Paper Reading

Read the paper itself and distinguish author claims from your interpretation.

Capture:

- research question, motivation, and claimed contribution;
- assumptions, definitions, and scope;
- method, data, baselines, metrics, and experimental setup;
- main results with table/figure/section references;
- limitations acknowledged by the authors;
- threats to validity, missing comparisons, and reproducibility gaps;
- what would be needed to reproduce or extend the work.

Check whether the abstract's claims match the results. Inspect figures and appendices when they carry material evidence. Record page/section/table references for specific statements. If the full paper is unavailable, label the analysis as abstract/metadata-only. Do not infer statistical significance, causality, or generalization beyond what the paper reports.
