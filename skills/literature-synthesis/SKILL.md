# Literature Synthesis

Organize the field by research question, method, evidence, or chronology—not as a sequence of isolated summaries.

1. Build a claim matrix linking every important claim to one or more inspected sources.
2. Compare problem definitions, datasets, assumptions, baselines, metrics, and evaluation settings.
3. Identify consistent findings, disagreements, methodological differences, and missing evidence.
4. Separate established results, emerging evidence, author speculation, and your own interpretation.
5. Avoid comparing numbers across incompatible datasets, splits, protocols, or hardware without qualification.
6. Include null/negative findings and limitations when relevant; do not cherry-pick only supportive papers.
7. State where evidence is sparse, outdated, or inaccessible.

Deliver a structured synthesis, source matrix, key themes, open problems, and search limitations. Cite the primary source near each factual claim. A literature gap is not automatically a novel contribution; verify it against current work.
