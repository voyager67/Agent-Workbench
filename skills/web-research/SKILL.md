# Web Research

Turn the question into a focused evidence-gathering task.

1. Clarify the decision, scope, geography, time window, and facts that would change the answer.
2. Search with multiple targeted queries. Prefer primary sources such as official documentation, standards, filings, datasets, release notes, and original statements.
3. Use reputable secondary sources for context and independent confirmation. Treat forums and social posts as experience reports, not authoritative facts.
4. Open the source and verify the exact claim. Do not treat a search snippet, title, or AI summary as evidence that the full source supports it.
5. For changing facts, record publication/update date separately from the date an event took place; confirm the current status with a recent authoritative source.
6. Compare sources for contradictions, scope differences, incentives, and missing context. Seek independent corroboration for consequential claims.
7. Maintain a source log with URL, publisher/author, relevant date, access date, supported claim, and confidence.
8. Separate sourced facts, reasoned inferences, recommendations, and unresolved unknowns in the final answer.

Deliver a concise answer with direct citations near factual claims, relevant alternatives, and explicit uncertainty. Do not invent URLs, quotes, figures, dates, or citations; do not claim to have read inaccessible material. Respect paywalls, access controls, privacy, and tool limits.
