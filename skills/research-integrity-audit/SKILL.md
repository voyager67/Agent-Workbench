# Research Integrity Audit

Trace claims back to evidence and test whether the evidence supports the wording.

1. Extract important factual, numerical, causal, comparative, and novelty claims.
2. Map each claim to primary sources, raw data, code, logs, tables, or figures.
3. Verify citations, quotations, publication status, and bibliographic metadata.
4. Recompute or independently inspect key numbers when the data and code are available.
5. Check that measured, simulated, estimated, and illustrative values are labeled distinctly.
6. Inspect split leakage, metric definitions, cherry-picking, missing baselines, multiple comparisons, and unexplained exclusions.
7. Compare paper text, tables, figures, abstract, and supplementary material for consistency.
8. Report unsupported claims, unverifiable artifacts, limitations, and the evidence required to resolve them.

Do not silently rewrite the findings to make them appear stronger. Do not claim plagiarism detection, statistical validation, or scientific correctness beyond the checks actually performed. Human domain review remains necessary.
